Forty versions of one glass: lighting notes from Halo Drift.

Kaja VollmerJanuary 18, 2027

The glass ring in Halo Drift is on screen for forty seconds and took forty versions. Most of them were wrong in ways that looked right at first, which is the most dangerous kind of wrong in look development.

Aurelo's brief was one word: hush. A new pair of over-ear headphones, no voice-over, no feature list, and a launch date in March. I proposed a single object to carry the feeling, a thick ring of glass the headphones drift through, bending the light around them the way good noise cancelling bends the room away.

Version one: too much glass

The first ring was beautiful and useless. Real glass at that thickness throws caustics everywhere, and the floor lit up like a disco. It was the ring the film was about, not the headphones. The first rule came out of that week: caustics never touch the product. They can dance on the floor, but the ear cups stay calm.

Light it in clay first

From version twelve on, every lighting change was made with the glass switched off. Three large softboxes and one thin strip, judged on grey clay, so the light could be read on its own before the glass started lying about it. If a setup did not work in clay, it never worked in glass.

The second rule: one hard light, everything else soft. The hard key gives the glass its edges; the soft boxes give the headphones their shape. Two hard lights and the ring starts to look like a chandelier.

Most of the versions were wrong in ways that looked right at first. That is the most dangerous kind of wrong.

Turn the rainbow down

Dispersion, the rainbow at the edges of real glass, is the first thing everybody falls in love with. By version thirty I had it at a third of the physically correct value. It still reads as glass, but the colour stays a whisper at the rim instead of a shout across the frame.

  • Caustics never touch the product.
  • One hard light, everything else soft.
  • Physically correct is a starting point, not the answer.

Those three rules will start my next glass job. The forty versions are on a drive in the studio, and I look at version one whenever I am sure I have got something right.
