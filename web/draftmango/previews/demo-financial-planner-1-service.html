Know your date. Then the paycheck after it.

What We Work Out

Six questions, answered in writing.

Retirement planning isn't one number. It's six decisions that change each other, so we answer them together and revisit them every year.

  1. 01

    When could you stop?

    Your work-optional date, tested against markets, inflation and the life you plan to live.

  2. 02

    What can you spend?

    A monthly paycheck from your savings, with guardrails that tell you when to spend more or less.

  3. 03

    Which account first?

    The order to draw from taxable, pre-tax and Roth accounts so lifetime tax stays low.

  4. 04

    When to claim Social Security?

    Claiming ages for both of you, compared side by side, including the survivor benefit.

  5. 05

    Roth conversions or not?

    How much to convert in the low-income years between your last paycheck and required withdrawals.

  6. 06

    Health cover before 65?

    Marketplace plans, subsidies and HSAs for the years before Medicare begins.

  • Age 38 · Today

    Max both 401(k)s, open two 529s, fix the cash buffer at six months.

  • Age 45

    College starts. 529s cover four years in-state; saving keeps going.

  • Age 52 · Work optional

    The paycheck switches to savings; Roth conversions fill the low brackets.

  • Age 67

    Social Security for Emma; Jack waits to 70 for the larger survivor benefit.
