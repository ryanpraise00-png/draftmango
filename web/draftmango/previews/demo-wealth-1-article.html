Gifting before April: an inheritance tax checklist.

Seven allowances most families never use, and the order to use them in before the tax year ends.

By Tom Ashby, Estate & Tax Planner24 September 20268 min read

Most families we meet worry about inheritance tax. Far fewer use the allowances that would shrink it. The rules aren't hidden — they're just easy to forget until the end of the tax year, when there's no time left to act.

This checklist runs through what you can give away each year without any inheritance tax at all, in roughly the order we'd use them. None of it needs a trust or a solicitor. Most of it needs a diary note and a short conversation.

Start with the allowances you already have

Everyone can give away £3,000 each tax year free of inheritance tax. If you didn't use last year's, you can carry it forward once — so a couple who missed last year could give £12,000 before 5 April.

On top of that come the smaller exemptions, which add up faster than people expect.

Before 5 April

  • Annual exemption: £3,000 each, plus last year's if unused.
  • Small gifts: up to £250 to as many people as you like.
  • Wedding gifts: £5,000 to a child, £2,500 to a grandchild.
  • Gifts from income: regular, from surplus income, with no limit.
  • Spouse or civil partner: gifts between you are exempt.

The quiet power of gifts from income

The least known exemption is often the most valuable. If you give regularly from income you don't need — not from savings — those gifts fall outside your estate straight away, however large they are.

The test is simple to describe and important to record: the gifts must be regular, come from income, and leave you able to live as you normally do. A short note each year, kept with your will, does most of the work.

The best gifting plans aren't clever. They're written down and repeated every year.

The seven-year clock

Larger gifts to people are usually free of inheritance tax if you live for seven years after making them. If you don't, and the gifts were above your £325,000 nil-rate band, the tax falls on a sliding scale.

That's why we often suggest giving earlier rather than later, and why a simple life policy written in trust can cover the tax for the seven years in between.

Pensions from April 2027

Until now, most pensions have sat outside your estate. From April 2027, unused pensions are due to count towards it. For many families that reverses the old advice to spend other savings first and leave the pension untouched.

If your plan relied on passing on a pension, it's worth a fresh look this year rather than next.

What to do this month

  1. List the gifts you've made since last April, with dates.
  2. Check whether last year's £3,000 is still available to carry forward.
  3. Decide on any regular gifts from income, and start a simple record.
  4. Ask your adviser how the 2027 pension changes affect your plan.

— Tom Ashby, Bristol
