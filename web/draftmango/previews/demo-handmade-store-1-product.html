The Mug

No two alike, and that is the point

Iron in the clay rises through the glaze in the kiln, so every mug comes out with its own scatter of speckles. Yours will be the one in the photos’ family, not its twin.

Made by Ida Lund

A garage in Sellwood, one wheel and a lot of clay

“I throw about forty mugs on a good day and keep maybe thirty-five. The handle is the last thing I do and the first thing you feel, so I pull every one by hand.”

Ida started on a borrowed wheel in 2014 and was one of the first twelve makers on Division Street in 2019. Every mug in this listing came out of her electric kiln in the last month.

How It’s Made

From clay to your table

A Kiln & Co. mug takes about three weeks, most of it spent drying slowly and waiting for the kiln.

  1. 01Wedge the clay

    Kneaded by hand to push out every pocket of air, so nothing cracks in the kiln.

  2. 02Throw and pull

    Thrown on the wheel, trimmed once it is leather-hard, then given a handle pulled by hand.

  3. 03Dip the glaze

    After a first bisque firing, every piece is dipped in one of three house glazes.

  4. 04Fire it slowly

    Fired to 2,232°F overnight and opened the next morning, still warm, then signed.
