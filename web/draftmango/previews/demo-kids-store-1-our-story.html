Our Story

Made to be handed down.

Draftmango Kids began with one cardigan that outlasted three cousins. Ten years on, we still make every piece so the next child gets it soft.

Two parents, one kitchen table and a box of hand-me-downs

When Greta had her first baby, the clothes that survived were the old ones: a cardigan her aunt knitted, a cord dungaree on its third owner. The new things pilled, shrank and went in a bag by spring.

So in 2016, Greta Lind and Jonah Hale started making the clothes they wished they could buy: organic cotton washed twice before it ships, knits linked by hand, solid-beech toys finished with plant oils. Small batches, in Minneapolis, built for a second and a third child.

Today a team of nineteen knits, sews, sands and wraps every order here, and the Trade-In program brings outgrown pieces home so they can start again.

Greta & Jonah Founders, Draftmango Kids

Since 2016

Ten years of things worth handing down

  1. 2016

    Forty cardigans from a kitchen table

    Greta knitted the first Buttercup Cardigans at home; they sold out at a Saturday market before lunch.

  2. 2017

    A shop on Linden Avenue

    A corner for trying sizes on, room for a stroller and a basket of toys by the window.

  3. 2019

    Trade-In begins

    Outgrown pieces come home, get cleaned and mended, and go on to the next family. 41,000 so far.

  4. 2021

    The toy workshop moves out of the garage

    Jonah’s beech rings and pull-along foxes get a bench of their own, one street from the shop.

  5. 2023

    The knitting studio

    Every knit finished and linked by hand under one roof: three rooms, one street apart.

  6. Today

    Nineteen of us, newborn to eight

    Knits, sleepwear, wooden toys and the nursery, and every order still gift-wrapped by hand, free.
