01 · Wind

Onshore wind, on working farmland.

Turbines sited for steady wind, roads a blade can travel and fields that stay in crops — about an acre out of each section.

  • 3.5–4.2 MW turbines, 1,500 ft from any home
  • Two years of wind data before a layout
  • Technicians who live in the county

02 · Solar

Solar that follows the sun.

Trackers tilt each row from east to west; native grasses and a neighbor's flock keep the ground in use.

  • 5–7 acres per megawatt, 35-year design life
  • 6,800 acres grazed by sheep
  • Most new plants paired with batteries
More on Solar

03 · Storage

Batteries for the evening peak.

Charged on midday sun and night wind, emptied in the hours after work — one full cycle a day.

  • Four hours at full output
  • Lithium iron phosphate, NFPA 855 spacing
  • Yearly drills with the local fire department
More on Storage

04 · Grid Services

A steadier grid, around the clock.

From the Minneapolis operations center we run every plant and sell the grid what it needs second by second: frequency response, voltage support and capacity for the hottest afternoons.

  • Operators on shift 24 hours, 365 days
  • Batteries respond in under a second
  • Forecasts for every turbine and row

How a Plant Comes to Life

Seven years to build, thirty to run.

Our 7.6 GW pipeline sits somewhere along this line. Most of it is still in the first three stages.

  1. Year 0

    Prospect

    Wind maps, sun hours and a substation with room on it.

  2. Years 1–2

    Lease

    Kitchen-table meetings and an option agreement with each family.

  3. Years 2–5

    Study & Permit

    Wildlife, sound and grid studies, then the county and state.

  4. 12–18 months

    Build

    Local crews first; roads and drainage repaired as we go.

  5. 30 years

    Operate

    Lease payments every year, then the land restored.
