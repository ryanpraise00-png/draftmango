Social Search Is the New Homepage

Lena Fischer Head of Strategy

Ask anyone under 30 where they'd look for a new skincare routine, a weekend in Lisbon or the best budgeting app, and most won't say Google. They'll open TikTok or Instagram, type three words into the search bar and scroll until a face they trust gives them the answer.

For brands, that changes what a post is for. It isn't only a moment in someone's feed any more. It's a page that can be found — next week, and next spring.

People search where they scroll

The habit started with recipes and outfits, and it has spread to almost every category we work in. When we audit a new client's account, the first thing we pull now is search data: what people type, which videos come up, and who made them. More often than not, the top results for a brand's own category belong to creators, competitors — or nobody at all.

That last one is the opportunity. A search with no good answer is an empty shelf, and the platforms are actively looking for posts to put on it.

What a search-ready post looks like

None of this means writing for robots. The posts that rank are still the ones people watch to the end. They simply make it easy for the platform to understand what they're about:

  1. Say the keyword in the first line. If the video is about oat milk for coffee, say "oat milk for coffee" out loud in the first three seconds.
  2. Put it on screen. A short text overlay with the same words helps the viewer and the algorithm at once.
  3. Answer one question per video. One clear question, one clear answer. Save the rest for the next post.
  4. Pin a comment with the answer. It gives the platform one more line to read, and gives skimmers the answer straight away.

Your next customer is typing your category into TikTok right now.

We don't treat this as a separate workstream. Search-ready habits go into every brief, every script and every creator's one-pager, so they cost nothing extra to make.

How we measure it

Views alone won't tell you whether search is working. Every weekly report we send tracks three numbers: the share of views that come from search, profile visits from search, and the terms that brought people in. When a term starts to climb, we make more posts that answer it. When one stalls, we look at the first line before we look at anything else.

Social search won't replace your website, but it has already become the first page many customers see. Treat every post as if it might be someone's homepage — because for a lot of people, it already is.
