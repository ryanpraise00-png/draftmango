The audit committee agenda for Q4: five items chairs keep adding.

Cyber disclosure, going-concern judgments and the auditor's own AI tools are on more agendas this year. What to ask, and what good answers sound like.

Michael RevellAudit & Assurance, New York

Published
Oct 14, 2026
All Insights

Every autumn we sit in more than a hundred audit committee meetings. This year the agendas grew in the same five places, whatever the company's size or sector.

None of these items is new on its own. What is new is how much time committees now give them, and how specific their questions have become.

1. Cyber incidents and what must be disclosed

Listed companies now report material cyber incidents within days. Committees want to know who decides what is material, how fast that decision reaches the board, and whether the process has been tested.

2. Going concern, earlier than before

Higher rates have put refinancing dates inside the twelve-month window for more companies. Ask management for the covenant forecast in October, not in February, and ask what would have to go wrong for the assessment to change.

The best committees ask the going-concern question before the auditor does.

3. The auditor's own use of AI

Chairs ask us how our tools read contracts and journal entries, and who reviews what they flag. It is a fair question. Our answer: every exception the tool finds is reviewed by a person, and the partner signs nothing a machine concluded alone.

4. Revenue in long contracts

Multi-year contracts with variable pricing remain the most common source of adjustments we propose. Committees increasingly ask to see the five largest judgments, not a summary of the policy.

5. Succession in the finance team

The quietest risk on the list. A controller leaving in January can cost a company its filing date. Ask who covers each key reconciliation and when that person last did it.

Questions to take to your next meeting

  • Who decides whether a cyber incident is material, and how long did it take last time?
  • What is the earliest date on which a covenant could be breached?
  • Which of the auditor's findings came from automated tools, and who reviewed them?
  • What are the five largest revenue judgments this year?
  • Who would close the books if the controller left tomorrow?
