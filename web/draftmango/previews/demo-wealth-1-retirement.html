Retirement planning

Stop work when you choose, not when the numbers say.

We turn your pensions and savings into an income that lasts — and show you, in advance, when you can afford to stop.

What retirement asks

Three questions, answered early.

  1. When can I stop?

    We model your finances to age 100 and find the year the numbers allow.

  2. How much can I spend?

    A yearly income you can rely on, with room for the good years early on.

  3. In what order?

    Which pot to draw from first, so less of your income goes in tax.

Your retirement income

One income, from several pots.

Most people retire with a State Pension, a workplace pension or two and some savings. Drawn in the right order, the same pots can pay noticeably more each year.

Old workplace pensions

Four jobs, four pensions. One clear view.

We trace every pension you've had and compare what each one costs and offers. Then we recommend what to combine — and what to leave alone.

  • Lost pensions found for you
  • Charges and guarantees checked line by line
  • Transfers handled from start to finish

Moving a pension isn't always right. Some carry guarantees worth keeping, and we'll tell you if staying put is better.

The run-up

The five years before you stop.

The best retirements are planned well before the leaving drinks.

  1. 5 yrs

    Set the Date

    Model the year you could stop, and what it would take.

  2. 3 yrs

    Tidy the Pots

    Combine old pensions and top up while you're earning.

  3. 1 yr

    Build a Cash Buffer

    Two years of spending set aside, so markets can't rush you.

  4. Day 1

    Switch On the Income

    Your first monthly payment lands, in the order we agreed.

  5. Each yr

    Review and Adjust

    Spend more in good years, a little less in lean ones.

Pension questions

The ones we hear most often.

From age 55 for most private pensions, rising to 57 from April 2028. The State Pension starts later, at your State Pension age.
Usually up to a quarter of your pension, capped at £268,275 for most people. Taking it all at once isn't always wise — we'll show you the options.
Often a bit of both: an annuity for the bills you must pay, drawdown for flexibility. The right mix depends on your health, your plans and your appetite for risk.
From April 2027, most unused pensions are due to count towards your estate. That changes which pots are best spent first, so it's worth a fresh look.
