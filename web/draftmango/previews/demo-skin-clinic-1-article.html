Sunscreen in the Sonoran sun: what actually works

Three hundred days of sun a year. The filters, the amounts and the habits that protect your skin here.

Dr. Hannah Lee, MDMedical Director

October 2, 2026

In Scottsdale the UV index sits above 8 from April to September, and above 3 even on a bright January morning. Sunscreen here is not a beach habit; it is the single treatment that does the most for your skin.

Choose the filter you will wear

Mineral (zinc oxide) and modern chemical filters both protect well. What matters is broad spectrum, SPF 30 or more for daily life and SPF 50 for long days outdoors, and a texture you are happy to put on every morning. A tinted mineral sunscreen also blocks some visible light, which helps if you are prone to pigment.

Use more than feels natural

Most of us apply a third to a half of the amount used in testing. For the face and neck that is about half a teaspoon, or two finger-lengths. Under that, an SPF 50 behaves more like an SPF 15.

“The best sunscreen is the one you reapply at lunchtime.”

Reapply, and plan around noon

Reapply every two hours outdoors and after swimming or sweating. A powder or mist SPF over make-up is better than nothing. Between 10 am and 4 pm, shade, a wide hat and sunglasses do as much as any bottle.

Our Three Rules

  1. SPF 30 every morning, SPF 50 outdoors.
  2. Half a teaspoon for face and neck.
  3. Reapply every two hours in the sun.

After a treatment

After a peel, microneedling or IPL your skin is more sensitive to light for two to four weeks. We send every patient home with a mineral SPF 50 and ask them to skip long hours outdoors until the review.

This article is general information, not medical advice. Ask your clinician about your own skin.
