Push fatigue is back: how we got past MFA four times.

IdentityOct 7, 20266 min readBy Nora Lindgren

Four times this year, on four different clients, the same trick got our red team past multi-factor authentication. None of it needed malware. All of it needed a tired person and a phone.

Push-based MFA was meant to end the stolen-password problem, and for the most part it did. But a push prompt asks a single question — was this you? — and people answer questions to make them go away. Send enough of them at the right hour and someone says yes.

Here's what those four engagements looked like, what stopped us on a fifth, and the three changes we now recommend to every client on push MFA.

What we found

In each case we started with a password from an old breach list or a convincing login page. The second factor was the only thing left in the way.

Findings across four red team engagements2026

  1. Critical Push prompt approved after repeated requests 9.1 Fixed
  2. High No number matching on the authenticator 8.0 Fixed
  3. High No alert on a burst of denied prompts 7.2 Fixed
  4. Medium Sign-ins allowed from any country 5.3 Open

Why it still works

Timing does most of the work. Prompts sent at 11 p.m. or just before the morning stand-up were approved far more often than ones sent mid-afternoon. In one case the approval came after the ninth prompt; in another, after a friendly call from "IT" explaining that the prompts were part of a migration.

On the fifth engagement we didn't get in. That client had turned on number matching, so a prompt meant nothing without the code on the login screen, and their SOC was alerting on bursts of denied pushes. We gave up after two hours.

Three changes worth making this week

  1. Turn on number matching. It's a setting in every major identity provider and it ends blind approvals.
  2. Alert on denied bursts. Five denied prompts in ten minutes is an attack, not a mistake.
  3. Move admins to phishing-resistant keys. Start with the twenty accounts that would hurt most.

The detection we use

If your sign-in logs land in Microsoft Sentinel, this is the query our SOC runs. Adjust the threshold to your noise level.

SigninLogs
| where TimeGenerated > ago(1h)
| where ResultType == "500121"   // MFA denied by user
| summarize Denied = count() by UserPrincipalName, bin(TimeGenerated, 10m)
| where Denied >= 5

None of this replaces training, but it makes training the second line of defence instead of the only one.
