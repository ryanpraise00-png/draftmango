Burst pipes: what your home policy pays for, and what it doesn't

A sudden burst is covered. A slow drip usually isn't. Here's where the line sits, what to do in the first hour, and the $4 add-on most homes should have.

Megan RyderAgent, Home & Auto Oct 14, 2026 · 5 min read

Every winter our claims line hears the same call: water coming through a ceiling, a cabinet full of it, a basement turning into a pond. The good news is that most of those claims are paid. The catch is a single word in your policy: sudden.

Sudden or slow: the line that decides it

Home policies pay for water damage that is sudden and accidental: a pipe that freezes and splits, a washing machine hose that bursts, a water heater that gives way. What they don't pay for is damage that builds up: a joint that drips for months, grout that lets water behind the tiles, a roof that has been letting in a little rain every storm.

The pipe itself is a repair job, like any other part of the house wearing out. The policy pays for what the water ruined: floors, walls, cabinets, your things, and a hotel if the house can't be lived in while it dries.

Common water damage and whether a standard home policy covers it
What happened Usually covered?
A pipe bursts in a freeze Yes
The washing machine hose splits Yes
A slow leak under the sink, for months No
Water backs up through a floor drain With the water backup add-on
The river rises into the basement Only with a flood policy

The first hour after a burst

  1. Turn off the water at the main valve. Find it before you need it: usually where the line enters the house, near the meter.
  2. Turn off the power to any room where water is near outlets or the panel.
  3. Take photos and video before you move anything, then lift what you can out of the wet.
  4. Call the claims line, (800) 555-0142. A person answers; a drying company can be on its way the same night.
  5. Keep every receipt: fans, a plumber, a hotel. Reasonable costs to stop more damage are part of the claim.
“The claims that go wrong are rarely the big ones. They're the slow leaks nobody noticed. Ten minutes under the sinks each autumn saves a lot of grief.”Megan Ryder, Agent

Stopping it before it starts

  • Insulate pipes in the garage, crawl space and outside walls
  • Keep the heat at 55°F or higher when you're away in winter
  • Replace washing machine hoses every five years, with braided steel
  • Put a $25 water sensor under the sinks and the water heater

Water backup cover: about $4 a month. It pays when drains or a sump pump back up into the house, which a standard policy leaves out. See what home cover includes.
