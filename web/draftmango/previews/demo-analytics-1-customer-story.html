The problem: a trial nobody finished

Fernway sells planning software to small agencies. Sign-ups were healthy, but fewer than one in four trial teams ever built a plan with real dates in it, and almost none of the rest ever came back.

The team had theories. Some blamed the import step, some the empty first screen, some pricing. What they didn’t have was a way to tell which theory was true without a month of tagging work.

Week one: one funnel, four steps

Autocapture was on the morning they installed the snippet. By the afternoon, Ellie Marsh, Fernway’s head of product, had a four-step funnel from sign-up to first shared plan, split by how each team arrived.

The answer was not where anyone expected. Import worked fine. Teams were stalling one step later, on an empty timeline that asked them to invite colleagues before they had anything to show.

Weeks two to four: watch, then change one thing

Session replay made the stall obvious. People scrolled the empty timeline, opened the invite dialog, closed it and left. Nobody wanted to invite a colleague into an empty room.

The fix was small. New teams now land on a sample plan built from their own imported tasks, and invites move to after the first plan is saved.

We had argued about onboarding for a year. The funnel ended the argument in an afternoon, and the replays told us what to build.

Ellie Marsh, Head of Product, Fernway

Weeks five and six: make the habit stick

With the leak fixed, the team turned to retention. A cohort chart showed that teams who shared a plan in week one were still active three months later twice as often.

So the product now nudges that moment, and Draftmango Analyst flags any week when new cohorts share less than usual. The first alert arrived on a Tuesday and pointed at a broken email template.

What’s next

Fernway is moving its event data into its own warehouse this quarter, so finance can read the same numbers as product. Same events, same definitions, one more team at the table.
