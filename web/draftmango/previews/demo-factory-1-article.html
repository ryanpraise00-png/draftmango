Five new 5-axis machines go live.

Twelve weeks from the first truck to the first good part, and what it means for lead times on aerospace housings.

Anna WeberEngineering Manager

Date
Sep 15, 2026
Plant
Plant 1 · Wyoming
Read
4 minutes

In June five 5-axis machining centers arrived at Plant 1 on eleven trucks. By the end of August all five were cutting production parts, and the 5-axis cells now count twelve machines.

We bought them for one reason: aerospace housings were waiting up to three weeks for a 5-axis spindle. With the new cells, first articles on those parts are back to three to four weeks from order, and production lots ship on the schedule we quote.

What we bought

Five trunnion-table machines with a 30 × 24 × 20 in work envelope, 20,000 rpm spindles and 120-tool magazines. Two of them sit on a shared six-pallet system, so long cycles run through the night shift without an operator at the door.

Twelve weeks, week by week

  • Weeks 1–3: foundations poured and cured for the two pallet machines.
  • Weeks 4–6: rigging, leveling and the coolant tie-in to the central system.
  • Weeks 7–9: probing, ballbar tests and a thermal warm-up routine written for each machine.
  • Weeks 10–12: first articles on six part numbers, all approved on the first submission.
“We did not buy speed. We bought the hours that aerospace parts were spending in a queue.”Anna Weber, Engineering Manager

What it means for an RFQ

If you have a 5-axis part on hold for lead time, send it now. We are quoting first articles in three weeks on the new cells through the end of the year, with DFM notes in 48 hours as usual.

Four machinists moved up from the vertical cells to run them, and two apprentices from the class of 2026 took their places.
