One designer, the whole job.

I'm Nora Ashby. Since 2015 I have run a design practice of exactly one person, and I intend to keep it that way.

I went independent so that the person you brief is the person who does the work.

I spent my first seven years in agencies in London, most of them as the designer three seats away from the client. Briefs reached me as summaries of summaries. Good ideas were lost on the way in, and good reasons were lost on the way out.

In 2015 I left with one client and three months of rent. The plan was simple: take fewer projects, sit in every conversation and do all of the work myself, from the first call to the last exported file.

Eleven years and 140 projects later the plan has not changed. What has changed is what I know: that a clear price calms a project more than any workshop, that unfinished work shown every Friday beats one perfect reveal, and that most brands need fewer things, drawn better.

How I work

Four habits I have kept since the first project. Clients tend to notice them in this order.

  1. Two directions, argued properly

    Not five logos to choose between. Two routes, each with its reasons, and the one I would bet on.

  2. A review every Friday

    You see the work each week in the state it is in. No big reveal, no surprise in week seven.

  3. A fixed quote, never hours

    You know the price before we start. If the scope changes we talk first, and the number changes second.

  4. Files you own

    Every source file, licence and login is yours in week eight. Nothing is held back to keep you coming back.
