The brief

Meridian measures the air on 400 street corners. Its old website explained the sensors and forgot the people who breathe.

The product had outgrown its site. City teams were buying it, parents and runners were opening the app every morning, and the homepage still led with a hardware diagram. Hanna's team asked for one thing: a visitor should understand what Meridian does for them before they scroll.

There was a second, quieter request. The open-data pages were the reason researchers trusted the numbers, and nobody could find them.

The approach

I started with the sentence, not the layout: “Know the air on your street, today.”

Everything else follows that line. The homepage opens with today's reading for the visitor's own district. The map became the second screen instead of the first, and the sensor story moved to a page for the people who actually buy hardware.

The blue is Meridian's own, used once per screen for the thing that matters. Every other surface is a pale grey taken from an overcast sky, so a bad-air day is the only loud moment on the site.

The screens

One system, three sizes

The marketing site and the app now share their parts: the same reading card, the same chart, the same eleven colours. Meridian's two developers build a new page from the kit in an afternoon, without asking me.
