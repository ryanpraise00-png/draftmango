Capability 01

Operating models leaders can run.

Decision rights, spans and layers, and the few roles that carry the strategy, signed off by your leaders before anyone is moved.

10 weeks

To a first result. 12–20 weeks end to end.

Structure follows the strategy, not the org chart.

We start from the decisions the strategy needs, not from boxes. Who decides, who is consulted, and which sixty roles carry most of the value. Only then do spans, layers and reporting lines follow.

  • Decisions that take days, not months
  • Two to three fewer layers, on average
  • Role cards leaders actually use
  • A transition your people can follow

What You Hold at the End

Four documents, all in use.

Nothing here is a slide for the shelf. Each one is owned by a named leader on your side.

  • Decision-Rights Map

    The forty decisions that matter, each with one owner and a clock.

  • Spans & Layers

    The target shape, layer by layer, with the cost of today’s.

  • Sixty Role Cards

    Purpose, decisions and measures for the roles that carry the strategy.

  • Transition Plan

    Who moves when, how it is told, and what stays the same.

How It Runs

Twenty weeks, five steps.

Leaders decide at step four, not before. Nobody is moved until the design is signed.

  1. Weeks 1–2

    Ambition

    What the strategy asks of the organisation, in the top team’s words.

  2. Weeks 3–5

    Diagnose

    Where decisions stall, layers pile up and cost hides. Interviews and data.

  3. Weeks 6–9

    Design

    Two or three options, each priced, each with its trade-offs written down.

  4. Week 10

    Decide

    The leadership team chooses one and signs it. The first result is on the table.

  5. Weeks 11–20

    Transition

    Roles filled, people told face to face, habits built. We hand over the office.
