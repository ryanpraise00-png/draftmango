Our story

Sam Whitfield and Jess Talbot opened Draftmango Kicks with sixty pairs in a room above a bike shop. Fourteen years on it is forty-odd labels and one wall of six hundred pairs, with the same rule: we only sell what we would fit you in.

A shop floor that knows shoes

Which labels run half a size small. Which foams go flat by spring. Why a wide forefoot needs a different last, not a bigger size. Fitting people properly is the whole job; the forty labels, the launches and the wall all came after.

How It Started

Sixty pairs and a borrowed till

Sam spent eight years fitting runners at a lakefront running shop. Jess bought footwear for a department store downtown and kept hearing the same thing from customers: nobody measures your feet anymore.

In 2013 they rented a room above a bike shop on Division St, built the shelves out of plywood and opened with sixty pairs from three labels, Corsa, Halyard and Tempo Lab, that agreed to sell to two people with no store. People climbed the stairs to be fitted, half size by half size, and came back with their friends.

Sam Whitfield & Jess TalbotFounders, Draftmango Kicks

One wall at a time

From a room up a flight of stairs to a corner on Milwaukee Ave, and the launches, the cleaning bar and the online store in between.

  1. 2013

    A room on Division St

    Sixty pairs, three labels, plywood shelves and a till borrowed from the bike shop downstairs.

  2. 2015

    The first raffle

    A Halyard restock drew a line round the block in January. The next release went to a raffle instead, and every launch since has opened 48 hours ahead, one entry per person.

  3. 2017

    Milwaukee Ave and the wall

    The corner store at 1418 N Milwaukee Ave in Wicker Park, and a wall of six hundred pairs, one of each, sorted by sport.

  4. 2019

    Online, with a try-on bar

    The store went online with two-hour pickup, and the try-on bar arrived: every half size of every style and a treadmill for a gait check.

  5. 2022

    Cleaning bar and our own line

    Hand cleaning from $25 while you wait, and the things we kept reaching for behind the counter under our own name: ribbed socks, a horsehair brush, a cap, a shoe bag.

  6. Today

    Forty labels, thirty-four people

    Shipping across the US, same day in Chicago before noon, and launch mornings that end in fair checkouts instead of overnight lines.

Labels, chosen one at a time

A new label spends a month on our own feet before it reaches the wall. If it runs small, scuffs early or comes from a reseller, it stays off.

Every pair, checked twice

No resale stock and no grey imports, ever. That is a promise we can only keep by doing it the slow way.

  1. Bought at the sourceStraight from the label or its own distributor, on paper, every season.
  2. Checked at the doorBox label, style code, stitching and size tags against the label’s sheet, pair by pair, before anything is shelved.
  3. Checked again at the counterThe fitter who hands you the box opens it with you. Online orders get the same look before they are sealed.
  4. Backed for 30 daysWorn indoors only and it does not feel right? Swap the size or bring it back.

The people on the floor

Thirty-four of us between the store, the cleaning bar and the packing room. Most started on a Saturday shift and never left.

“We hire people who can talk shoes all day and still be kind at 9 AM on a launch Saturday. The rest we can teach.”

Jess Talbot, co-founder
