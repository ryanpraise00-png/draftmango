Orra 9 Pro review: the quiet phone that finally gets the camera right

Three weeks, two continents, 4,000 photos. A flagship that stops chasing specs and starts taking pictures you keep.

Elio BreckenReviews Editor, Phones
Share

For five years, every flagship phone review has started the same way: a bigger number. More megapixels, more zoom, more frames per second. The Orra 9 Pro is the first phone in a long time that seems to have been designed by someone who actually looks at their photos afterwards.

It has fewer cameras than last year's model, not more. It has one large sensor, a sensible 3× telephoto and an ultra-wide you'll rarely use. And after three weeks in Lisbon and Portland, it's the phone I'd hand to anyone who asks which one to buy.

The camera, finally grown up

The main sensor is a full one inch across, the biggest Orra has fitted. In daylight you won't notice. At night you will: shadows stay clean instead of turning to mush, and the processing stops painting every face the same shade of orange.

It's the first phone in years where I stopped carrying my camera on weekends. Elio Brecken, Reviews Editor

Battery: the two-day phone is back

With the screen at 60% and the usual mix of maps, messages and photos, the Orra 9 Pro ended most days with 45% left. Our rundown test, a looping web script at 200 nits, lasted 26 hours and 40 minutes, three hours more than the best phone we tested last year.

  • Charging: 0 to 50% in 22 minutes with the 65 W brick, which is sold separately.
  • Heat: 41 °C at the back after 20 minutes of 4K video, the coolest flagship in our chart.
  • Updates: seven years of OS and security updates, written into the warranty.

What we'd change

The ultra-wide is soft at the edges, and at $999 the 128 GB base model is stingy. Spend the extra $100 on 256 GB. The new matte back also picks up fingerprints in the first week before it settles.
