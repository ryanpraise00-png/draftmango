Fenwick Energy · 3,800 people, Newcastle

Every decision came back to her desk.

The challenge. Claire Donovan had grown Fenwick's operations from two wind sites to eleven. Her directors still brought her every call, and her diary had no hour left for the board.

What we did. Nine months with Amelia Hart. The 360° showed her team waiting for permission she thought she had given; she set two goals with her CEO, and wrote a one-page note of what her directors now decide alone.

Six months in, my team stopped waiting for me to decide. That was the whole point.

Claire DonovanChief Operating Officer, Fenwick Energy

Calder & Ross · 600 engineers, Edinburgh

A new MD, and a team that wanted his job.

The challenge. Oliver Grant was hired from outside. Two of his five directors had applied for the role, and the first quarterly offsite ended early.

What we did. Six months with Tom Ashworth, who once ran an engineering firm himself. One conversation with each director in the first month, then a new contract for how the team decides.

Direct, warm and never generic. I left every session with one thing to do.

Oliver GrantManaging Director, Calder & Ross

Arden Biotech · 240 people, Cambridge

Weekly meetings, few decisions.

The challenge. Sam Turner's leadership team met every Monday for three hours. A choice between two clinical trials had been on the agenda for six weeks.

What we did. Seven months of Team Coaching with Sophie Lindqvist: two offsites, eight team sessions and four one-to-ones for Sam. The team now names an owner and a date before any item leaves the room.

We used to leave the room with three opinions. Now we leave with a decision.

Sam TurnerChief Executive, Arden Biotech
