Itinerary · Iceland · 12 Days

Iceland’s south coast in twelve unhurried days.

By Linnea Rosetti · Photos by Jasper Corrigan · 16 min read

The Route

Reykjavík 1–2 The Waterfalls 3–4 Vík 5–6 Skaftafell 7–9 Jökulsárlón 10–12 The Budget $

We have driven Iceland’s ring road twice, and both times we spent too many hours in the car. So the third time we did the opposite: one coast, five bases, and twelve days to see what happens when you stop rushing past the waterfalls on the way to the next one.

This is that route, written up with everything we’d keep and the one thing we’d change. It works from June to September in a small two-wheel-drive car, and the budget at the end is what we actually spent, receipts included.

Days 1–2Reykjavík: land, swim, sleep

Pick up the car at the airport, but don’t drive far. Spend the first evening at Vesturbæjarlaug, the neighborhood pool where half the city soaks after work, and the next morning walking the old harbour before the cruise crowds wake up.

Stock up at Bónus before you leave town. Rye bread, skyr, cheese and a bag of coffee will carry you through ten breakfasts and save around $25 a day.

Days 3–4Seljalandsfoss and Skógafoss

Base yourselves at a farm guesthouse between the two falls and visit each one twice: once at noon like everyone else, once at nine in the evening when the light goes gold and the car park empties.

Walk the first hour of the Fimmvörðuháls trail above Skógafoss. Twenty-two more waterfalls line the river, and almost nobody goes past the third.

Days 5–6Vík and the black beaches

Reynisfjara is beautiful and genuinely dangerous: the sneaker waves are real, so stay well back from the water line. Go at dawn, then drive ten minutes east to the quieter beach below Hjörleifshöfði.

Days 7–9Skaftafell: three nights, two glaciers

This is the stretch where slowing down pays off. Book a guided glacier walk on Falljökull, then spend a free day on the loop to Svartifoss and the viewpoint at Sjónarnípa, where the whole glacier tongue opens below you.

  • Glacier walk with crampons: about $120 each, three hours.
  • Svartifoss and Sjónarnípa loop: 4.5 miles, free, bring lunch.
  • Fill the tank in Kirkjubæjarklaustur: the next pump is 45 miles away.

Days 10–12Jökulsárlón and the long way back

Paddle among the icebergs in the morning, when the lagoon is still, then cross the road to Diamond Beach, where the ice washes up on the black sand. On the drive back west, stop at every waterfall you skipped on the way out.

The BudgetWhat two of us spent

Item For 12 days
Small car and full insurance $1,140
Fuel, 560 miles $310
11 nights, guesthouses and one cabin $1,920
Food, mostly self-catered $560
Glacier walk, kayak, pools $350
Total for two $4,280

The one thing we’d change: one fewer night in Vík, one more in Skaftafell. You’ll see.
