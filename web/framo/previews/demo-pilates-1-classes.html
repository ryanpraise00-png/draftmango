Where to Start

Three levels, read by the springs.

Every class shows its intensity as three bars. More bars means lighter springs and longer holds — less help from the machine, more from you.

  1. Gentle

    Your first month, a body coming back from injury, or a pregnancy. Heavier springs steady you while you learn.

    • Reformer Foundations
    • Pre/Postnatal Reformer

  2. Steady

    Open to everyone after one class. Your instructor gives a lighter or heavier option for most exercises.

    • Full Body Reformer
    • Tower
    • Mat & Barre Fusion

  3. Strong

    After ten or so classes. Light springs, the jump board, and fewer pauses between blocks.

    • Sculpt & Jump Board

Tower · Studio Two

Six people, springs overhead.

The tower is a frame at the head of the mat with springs that pull from above and a push-through bar. It assists as much as it resists, which makes it the class for stiff hips, a cranky back and anyone who wants their instructor close.

Pre/Postnatal Reformer · Studio Two

Adapted, trimester by trimester.

Eight reformers, lighter springs and Olivia, who changes the positions as your body does. Bring your doctor's okay to your first class.

Close to a regular class with the intensity eased: no deep twists, no breath holding, and a cool room if you're feeling queasy. Tell us — we'll keep it to ourselves.
Less time on your back, more side-lying and seated work, and the headrest raised. Springs get lighter as your balance shifts.
Breath, pelvic floor and the upper back that carries the load. Mostly seated and kneeling, with a box on the carriage so you never lie flat.
From six weeks with your doctor's clearance (longer after a C-section). We rebuild the deep core first and check for abdominal separation before anything else.
