Twelve hours in Marseille with one roll of film

Thirty-six frames, no second roll, from the fish market at six to the last ferry at nine.

Every spring I give myself one day in my own city with one roll of film and no second chance. Thirty-six frames is a strict editor. It makes you wait for the picture instead of collecting near misses, and it is the best cure I know for a winter of commissions.

The rules are simple. One camera, one lens, one roll. No phone pictures. If I miss something, it stays missed, and I write it down in the notebook instead.

Six o'clock, the old port

The fish market on the Quai des Belges starts before the light does. By six the tables are out, the boats are tied up and the first buyers are arguing gently about sea bream. I spend four frames here, which is already too many.

The trick at the market is to stand still. Walk around and every fishmonger poses; stay by one table for twenty minutes and they forget you.

Thirty-six frames is a strict editor. It makes you wait for the picture instead of collecting near misses.

Noon, the Calanques

By noon the light is flat and the city is loud, so I take the bus to the end of the line and walk to the coves. The water does the work here. Two frames of the same diver, one second apart, and I still can't choose.

Nine o'clock, the last ferry

I keep three frames for the evening, and as always I need five. The last one goes on the ferry back across the harbour, the city turning pink behind the boats. It is not the best frame on the roll. It is the one I will print.

The roll goes to the lab on Monday. I'll post the contact sheet here when it comes back, misses and all.
