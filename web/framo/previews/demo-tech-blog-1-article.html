Two-nanometer chips reach volume production a quarter early

Yields crossed 70% in September, two fabs confirmed. What that means for the phones and laptops you'll buy next autumn.

Sanna CallenNews Editor · 5 min read
Share

The first two-nanometer chips were supposed to reach phones in the spring of 2028. They will now arrive in time for next year's autumn launches, after two of the world's largest foundries confirmed this week that their 2 nm lines are in volume production, a full quarter ahead of plan.

The milestone matters less for the number, which is mostly marketing, than for what changed underneath it. These are the first mass-market chips built with gate-all-around transistors, where the gate wraps the channel on all four sides instead of three.

70%Yield on the leading line in September
−30%Power at the same speed vs. 3 nm
$30kEstimated cost of one 2 nm wafer

What you'll actually notice

Not speed. The chip designers we spoke to were consistent: almost all of the gain will be spent on battery life and heat. Expect next year's flagship phones to run about two hours longer on the same battery, and thin laptops to hold full speed for longer before throttling.

We could have shipped a faster chip. Everyone told us they'd rather have a cooler one. A chip architect at a phone maker, speaking on background

Why it came early

Two reasons, according to three people familiar with the lines. The new extreme-ultraviolet tools arrived two months ahead of their delivery window, and defect rates on the gate-all-around step fell faster than expected once the lines ran around the clock in July.

Who gets them first

Data centres, mostly. The first year's output is largely booked for AI accelerators, which pay more per wafer. Phones follow in autumn, laptops in the months after, and mid-range devices not before 2029.

That makes this a good year to buy a phone or laptop you're happy with, rather than waiting. The 2 nm generation will be better, but not by enough to justify living with something you dislike for another twelve months.
