    Seven ways to write help articles your agent can actually use.

    Short answers, one topic per page and real numbers. What we learned from 3,400 help centers.

    Rachel MorenoHead of Content Design

    GuidesOct 15, 20268 min read

    Your agent is only as good as what it reads. The help centers that get the best answers aren’t the longest. They’re the clearest.

    We looked at the articles behind 40 million conversations. The same seven habits came up again and again in the help centers with the highest resolution rates.

    1. One question per article

    “Shipping and returns” is two articles. When a page answers one question, the agent finds the right paragraph faster, and so do people.

    2. Put the answer first

    Start with the sentence a customer needs, then explain. The agent quotes the opening lines more than anything else on the page.

    3. Use real numbers

    “A few days” becomes “3 to 5 working days.” “A small fee” becomes “$4.” Vague words force the agent to hedge, and hedging reads as not knowing.

    4. Write the exceptions down

    Your team knows that gift cards can’t be refunded and that Alaska ships slower. If it isn’t written, the agent can’t know it either.

    “If a new teammate would need to ask, the agent will need to ask too.”

    Rachel Moreno

    5. Name things the way customers do

    If customers say “locker code” and your article says “collection PIN,” add both. Insights shows you the exact words people use.

    6. Keep one source of truth

    Two articles that disagree are worse than none. Retire old pages instead of adding new ones beside them.

    7. Review the gaps every week

    Each Monday, open the knowledge gaps report and write the top three missing articles. Twenty minutes a week moves resolution more than any setting.

    Before you publish

    • Does the first sentence answer the question?
    • Are times, prices and limits written as numbers?
    • Are the exceptions on the page?

    None of this is new. It’s what good support writers have always done. The difference is that now every improvement shows up in your resolution rate within days.

    Rachel MorenoLeads content design at Framo. Before that, she ran the help center for a travel company in six languages.
