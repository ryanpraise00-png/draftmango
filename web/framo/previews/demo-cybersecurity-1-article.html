Anatomy of a 6‑minute containment

A stolen password, a mailbox rule and a laptop in finance. Every step of a real credential attack, and what stopped it.

Tom ReillyVP, Security Operations

Field NotesOct 14, 20269 min read

At 02:14 on a Tuesday, a laptop nobody had seen before joined a freight company's network. Six minutes later, the person behind it was locked out for good.

Nothing about the night was unusual. No zero-day, no clever malware. Just a password bought on a forum and a person patient enough to use it quietly. That is how six in ten of the intrusions we handle begin.

02:14 · Something small

A new device enrolled under a finance manager's account. On its own, that happens a hundred times a day across our customers. The AI scored it low and kept watching.

02:15 · Three small things

Within a minute the same account created a mailbox rule that hid any message containing "invoice", then signed in from a country it had never used. Three quiet signals, one session. That is the moment a case is born.

02:16 · A person takes the case

Our AI linked the signals with 97% confidence and wrote down why. Then it did what it always does with a case like this: it handed it to a person. Aoife, three hours into her Dublin shift, had it open 40 seconds later.

"The AI tells me what it saw. I decide what it means. At two in the morning, that split is the whole job."

Aoife Brennan, Senior Analyst, Dublin

02:21 · Contained

She revoked every session on the account, forced a password reset, deleted the mailbox rule and isolated the laptop. The customer's IT director woke up to a one-page summary instead of a crisis.

What would have stopped it sooner

Nothing here needed new software. Three settings would have turned this from a six-minute case into a non-event.

Check these this week

  • Require MFA on the VPN and on every admin account
  • Alert on new mailbox forwarding and hiding rules
  • Block sign-ins from countries you never work in

Attackers rarely break in any more. They log in. The good news is that logging in leaves a trail, and a trail is something a patient team can follow at any hour.

Tom Reilly

VP, Security Operations. Tom runs both Framo SOCs from Dublin and still covers a night shift every month. Before Framo he led incident response for a European bank.
