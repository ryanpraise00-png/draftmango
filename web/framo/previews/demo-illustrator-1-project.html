Mural · Westshore Libraries · 2026

The whale wall.

A fourteen-metre sea for a children’s reading room, drawn with thirty young readers and painted over nine evenings and Sundays.

The Brief

A room the children would claim.

The reading room had one long, blank wall, beige since 1998. Westshore wanted a picture that would make the room feel like the children’s own, survive sticky hands and a decade of daylight, and go up without closing the library for a single day.

We agreed on three rules early: the children choose the story, the wall stays calm enough to read beside, and every animal is at a height where someone small can touch it.

01 · Drawn With the Kids

Thirty drawings, one sea.

One Saturday morning, thirty readers aged five to eleven drew the sea they wanted on the wall. Then they voted.

  • Whales won by a landslide, then ladders, then “a lot of small fish”
  • Four colours chosen by vote, seven more mixed from them
  • Three roughs back to the room, one pinned up for a week of comments

02 · On the Wall

Beige on Monday, sea by the Sunday after.

A chalk grid, a scaffold that rolled away at opening time, and paint mixed in the car park. Drag to compare.
