Case Study · Banking

A consent order, lifted eight months early.

How a $6.8B regional bank rebuilt its BSA/AML program in fourteen months, and closed every finding on the first review.

Client
Harborline Bank
Sector
Regional banking, $6.8B assets
Regulator
Federal Reserve, state
Engagement
14 months, 2024–2025
Practices
Compliance Programs, Internal Audit
Partner
Caroline Prescott

The challenge

After a 2023 examination, Harborline’s regulators issued a consent order covering transaction monitoring, customer due diligence and a backlog of 38,000 unreviewed alerts. The bank had two years to fix it, and a board that had heard “nearly done” before.

The team was capable but stretched. Policies described a program that the systems did not run, and nobody could show an examiner which control met which requirement.

Our approach

We started with a four-week assessment, mapping each article of the order to a control, an owner and a test. Then a team of six worked beside the bank’s BSA officer: retuning monitoring scenarios, clearing the alert backlog with a documented method, and rewriting procedures with the analysts who use them.

Every fix went through independent validation testing before it was reported as complete. The evidence binder was indexed to the order’s articles, so the examiners could test each one in an afternoon.

The result

The regulators terminated the order after fourteen months, eight months ahead of the deadline. At the next full-scope examination there were no repeat findings. Harborline kept Framo on retainer for quarterly testing and the 24-hour desk.
