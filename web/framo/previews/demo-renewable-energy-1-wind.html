Wind at Framo

Steady wind, roads that take a blade.

Wind was our first business and is still our largest. We look for ridges and plains where the wind blows steadily at hub height, a transmission line within reach, and county roads a 260-foot blade can travel.

From Mast to Power

Five to seven years, most of it listening.

The cranes are the short part. Before them come two winters of wind data, a layout drawn around every drainage line, and a county hearing.

  1. 01

    Measure

    Two years

    A wind mast and a lidar log every gust at hub height through two winters.

  2. 02

    Lay Out

    With each family

    Turbines go where the farmer can still turn a planter — at least 1,500 feet from any home.

  3. 03

    Permit

    One to two years

    Bird and bat surveys, a sound and shadow study, then the county and the state.

  4. 04

    Build

    12–18 months

    Roads, foundations, cable, then the cranes — with crews hired from the county first.

  5. 05

    Run

    Thirty years

    Technicians live nearby; the operations center in Minneapolis watches every blade.

Questions

What neighbors ask first.

Every study behind these answers is filed with the county and open to read.

At our minimum setback of 1,500 feet, about 40 decibels — a quiet library. The sound study models every home before a single turbine is placed.
We keep it under 30 hours a year at any home, and the turbine stops on its own when the sun sits at the wrong angle.
Two seasons of surveys shape the layout; in late summer the rotors stay still at low wind speeds, when bats fly most.
We repower with new turbines or take them down. A decommissioning bond, posted before construction, pays to remove every foundation to four feet below the plow line.
