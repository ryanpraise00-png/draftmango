Five signs an email isn't from who it says.

Phishing is still how most break-ins start. Here are the tells our security team sees every week, and what to do in the minute after you spot one.

Owen FischerHead of Security

SecurityOct 14, 20267 min read

Most of the break-ins we clean up didn't start with clever code. They started with a normal-looking email, opened on a busy afternoon.

Last year our team flagged 1,930 phishing attempts across our clients. The good news: almost all of them gave themselves away in one of five ways.

1. The sender is close, but not quite

“accounts@halvorsen-legal.co” instead of “halvorsenlegal.com”. Tap or hover on the name to see the real address. One letter off is the most common trick we see.

2. It's urgent, and it's about money

“Pay this today or the account closes.” Real suppliers and real banks don't work that way. Pressure is there to stop you from checking.

3. The link doesn't match the words

The button says “View invoice” but the address underneath leads somewhere else. If you wouldn't type that address yourself, don't click it.

What our desk sees

#48302 · Blocked in 2 min “Invoice overdue” from a look-alike domain

Forwarded by an office manager at 4:40 on a Friday. The domain was registered that morning.

4. It asks you to sign in

A shared document that needs your password again, or a mailbox that's “almost full”. Close the email and open the app the way you normally would instead.

5. It's from the boss, and it's odd

Gift cards, a quick wire transfer, “are you at your desk?” If your manager wouldn't usually ask for it by email, call them on the number you already have.

Nobody gets told off for forwarding a strange email. We'd rather check 100 safe ones than miss the one that isn't.

Owen Fischer

The one-minute habit

  • Don't click, don't reply
  • Forward it to the help desk
  • If you already clicked, call us now
  • Change nothing until we call back

Our clients get a two-minute training video each quarter and a practice phishing email now and then. Teams that do both click on half as many real ones by the end of the first year.
