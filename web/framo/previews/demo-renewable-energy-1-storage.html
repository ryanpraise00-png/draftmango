Storage at Framo

Power saved at noon, spent at supper.

A battery yard is a row of steel enclosures on a gravel pad, next to a substation. It fills when power is plentiful and cheap, and empties in the hours after work, when every stove and air conditioner comes on at once.

A Battery’s Day

One cycle, every day of the year.

A typical summer day at Coldwater, hour by hour. In between, the batteries answer the grid in under a second whenever a plant somewhere trips.

Midnight6 a.m.Noon6 p.m.Midnight

  • 10 a.m.–3 p.m. · Charge

    Midday solar is cheapest; the yard fills in about four and a half hours.

  • 5 p.m.–9 p.m. · Serve

    Four hours at 200 MW, through the peak, until demand falls off.

  • The rest · On Standby

    Held ready for frequency response, paid by the grid operator.

Safety

Watched cell by cell.

The most stable lithium chemistry made for the grid: it runs cooler and resists the chain reaction that makes other batteries burn.
Every enclosure stands apart from the next, tested so that a fault in one stays in one.
Temperature, voltage and gas are read thousands of times a minute; the operations center isolates a rack before a person would notice.
Before a site opens, and every year after, on site, with an emergency plan they keep a copy of.
