Grown for flavour, not for the truck

Fernhill grows two old varieties for us under open-sided tunnels in the Hudson Valley: a round red that holds its shape in a salad and a softer one that collapses into sauce. Both stay on the vine until they colour all the way to the shoulder.

They travel 110 miles in our chilled van — set a little warmer for tomatoes, which go mealy in the cold — and are packed loose in a paper bag, never on a plastic tray.

  • Keep them out of the fridge

    On the counter, stem side down and out of direct sun. Ripe ones keep three to four days.

  • Cut just before eating

    A serrated knife keeps the skin from tearing. Salt them ten minutes ahead for a salad.

  • Soft ones go in the pan

    Past their best for slicing? Blister them in olive oil for ten minutes — that's tonight's sauce.
