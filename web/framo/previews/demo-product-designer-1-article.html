Onboarding · 7 min read · March 2027

Onboarding is a promise, not a tour.

Product tours explain the buttons. Good onboarding keeps one promise from the store page, fast. What fourteen weeks with Tendwell taught me about the first ten minutes.

Every app makes a promise on its store page. Tendwell's was simple: share the care of Mom with your brother, without the group chat. Then the app opened and asked for her blood type.

That gap, between the promise and the first screen, is where most onboarding fails. Not because the screens are ugly, but because they ask for effort before they give anything back.

The tour is the wrong tool

When sign-up numbers drop, the usual fix is a tour: five swipeable cards that explain the tabs. Teams like tours because they are easy to ship. People skip them because they already know what a tab bar is.

A tour explains the product. Onboarding should prove it. The question for the first ten minutes is not "do they understand the app?" but "did the app keep its promise?"

Onboarding is not the start of the product. It is the product, compressed into ten minutes.

Find the smallest true moment

For Tendwell, the promise was shared care. The smallest real version of that is one task, owned by one sibling, seen by another. Everything we built for the first run served that moment and nothing else.

Eleven screens became five. Twenty-three required fields became two. Medications, doctors and insurance moved to the moment a task needs them, which for most families is the second week.

Three questions I now ask on every project

  1. What did the store page promise? Write it down in the words the store page uses.
  2. What is the smallest real version of it? One task, one message, one invoice sent.
  3. What can wait? Anything that is not needed for that moment moves later, or goes.

Key takeaways

  • Measure onboarding by the first real outcome, not by screens finished.
  • Ask for data at the moment it is needed, never before.
  • Test at the hour people really sign up. For Tendwell, that was 10 PM.

Three months after release, 78% of new caregivers finished the first run, up from 41%. The tour we had planned never shipped. Nobody missed it.
