A slow morning sequence for rainy days.

Twenty minutes, six shapes, no heat required — for the grey Portland mornings when the mat feels far away.

Hannah ColePrenatal specialist, 11 years teaching. Slow Flow Mon & Thu 9:30 am.

All teachers

Some mornings the rain starts before the alarm and the mat might as well be in another city. This is the sequence I give students for those days: short, warm, almost entirely on the floor, and kind to a stiff back.

You need a mat, two blocks (or two thick books) and a blanket. Put the kettle on first. By the time it boils you will be halfway through.

The sequence

  1. 01 Child’s pose, wide knees Two minutes. Let the forehead rest on a block or your hands. Breathe into the back of the ribs.
  2. 02 Cat and cow Ten slow rounds, moving with the breath, the spine one vertebra at a time.
  3. 03 Low lunge, each side Back knee down on a folded blanket, hands on blocks. Five breaths, then the other side.
  4. 04 Standing forward fold Knees soft, hold your elbows, sway a little. Roll up slowly at the end.
  5. 05 Supported bridge A block under the sacrum at its lowest height. Three minutes, arms wide.
  6. 06 Legs up the wall Five minutes or more. A blanket over you, eyes closed, the rain outside.

Why it works

Grey mornings make us fold inward: shoulders up, breath short. The sequence moves the spine in every direction once, opens the front of the hips that sat all night, and ends with the legs above the heart, which most bodies read as permission to settle.

“Do less than you think you should. The morning will meet you halfway.”

If you have twenty more minutes, add a Slow Flow class — Monday and Thursday at 9:30 am in the Cedar Room, where it never rains.
