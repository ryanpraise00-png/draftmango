How to read a loan offer before you sign it.

Interest rate, APR, factor rate, total cost. Which number to compare, which one lenders lead with, and a worked example you can check yourself.

Megan YarrowCo-Founder & Chief Credit Officer

Most loan offers put one number in large type. It is rarely the one you should compare. Before you sign anything, find three figures on the page: the interest rate, the APR and the total of payments. Together they tell you what the money really costs.

Three numbers on every offer

The interest rate is what the lender charges on the balance you owe. The APR (annual percentage rate) adds the fees, like an origination fee, and spreads them over the term, so two offers with different fees can be compared fairly. The total of payments is simply every payment added up.

  • Compare offers on APR, not on the interest rate.
  • Check the total of payments against what you are borrowing.
  • Ask whether the fee is taken out of the loan or added to it.

Factor rates: read them twice

Some lenders quote a factor rate, like 1.2, instead of an interest rate. It means you repay $1.20 for every dollar borrowed, no matter how fast you pay. Over six months, a 1.2 factor works out to an APR far above 20%. Ask for the APR in writing; any lender can give it to you.

A worked example

Here is a real-shaped offer for a $50,000 term loan over 12 months. The origination fee is taken from the loan, so $48,750 reaches your account.

Loan amount$50,000

Interest rate
19.9%
Origination fee (2.5%)
$1,250
Monthly payment × 12
$4,629.33
Total of payments
$55,551.96

APR24.83%

The interest rate says 19.9%. The APR says 24.83%, because the $1,250 fee is a cost of the money too, and because you only received $48,750. That is the number to put beside any other offer.

If an offer doesn’t show the APR, ask for it. If the lender won’t give it, that is your answer.

Before you sign

  1. Is there a penalty, or a lost discount, for paying early?
  2. Are payments daily, weekly or monthly, and from which account?
  3. What happens if a payment bounces?
  4. Who is the lender named on the agreement?
