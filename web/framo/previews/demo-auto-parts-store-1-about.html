Our Story

A technician and a parts manager, one counter

Wes Kendrick opened Framo Auto Service on Larimer Street in 2011. Every week neighbors asked to buy the pads or the battery he had just fitted to their car, so they could do the next job at home. In 2015 he asked Dana Haskell, who had run a dealer parts department for nine years, to build a proper counter at the front of the shop.

Dana stocked only what the technicians trusted, wrote the engine code on every box and refused to sell a part she could not match to the customer’s car. That counter became Framo Auto Parts: online since 2019, from our own warehouse since 2021, still answering the phone with technicians.

“A part that almost fits costs you a Saturday. We would rather spend two minutes on the phone than sell you the wrong rotor.”

Dana Haskell, Head of Parts

How We Check a Part

Four checks between the shelf and your driveway

  1. Chosen on the liftsA brand joins the shelves only after the service centre has fitted it for a season and the comebacks are counted.
  2. Matched to the engineEvery part number is tied to the year, model, engine code and, where it matters, the build date.
  3. Flagged for a personOrders with an unusual fit are checked by a parts specialist, who calls before anything ships.
  4. Scanned twice, sent the same dayPicked against the order, scanned again at packing, out by the afternoon run or on the counter by 4 PM.
