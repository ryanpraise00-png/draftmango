A Worked Example

When an S-corp starts to pay for itself.

One owner, $120,000 of net profit. As a sole proprietor, all of it carries self-employment tax. As an S-corp, only the salary does.

Sole proprietor

$16,955

Self-employment tax: 15.3% on 92.35% of the profit.

S-corp, $70,000 salary

$12,710

$10,710 payroll tax on the salary, plus about $2,000 for payroll and a second return.

What the owner keeps

$4,245

a year in payroll tax, before income and state taxes.

An illustration, not advice. The salary must be reasonable for the work, and state taxes, the qualified business income deduction and benefits change the result. We run it with your own numbers before anyone files Form 2553.
