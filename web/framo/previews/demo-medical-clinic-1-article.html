How many steps a day does your heart need?

Reviewed by Dr. Ryan Mitchell, MD

You have probably heard that ten thousand steps a day is the target. It is a fine number, but it began as a slogan for a pedometer, not as a medical finding. The good news is that your heart starts to benefit well before that.

What the research says

Large studies that follow people for years find that the risk of heart disease falls steadily as daily steps go up, and then levels off. For most adults the curve flattens somewhere around 7,000 to 9,000 steps a day. Older adults reach most of the benefit a little earlier.

Pace matters too. A part of your walk that is brisk enough to make talking a little harder adds more than the same steps taken slowly.

Start from where you are

Look at your average over the last week, not your best day. Add about a thousand steps a day, keep that for two weeks, then add another thousand. Small and steady beats a burst that hurts your knees.

  • Walk the first or last ten minutes of your commute.
  • Take phone calls on your feet.
  • After dinner, a short walk round the block helps blood sugar too.

When to talk to us first

If you have a heart condition, have had chest pain on exertion, or get unusually short of breath, check with your doctor before you increase your activity. A quick ECG or a stress test can tell us what is safe.

“Walking is the medicine I prescribe most. It is cheap, it has almost no side effects, and it works.”Dr. Ryan Mitchell, MD, Cardiology

Chest pain, pressure or tightness during a walk is not something to push through. Stop and call 911.

This article is general information, not a substitute for advice from your own doctor. Individual results vary.
