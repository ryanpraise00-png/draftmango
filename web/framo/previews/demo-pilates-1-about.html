A studio built around one machine.

Framo Reformer started with six reformers in one north-facing room on Sunset. Six years on there are twenty-four, and the room still matters more than the playlist.

Our Story

It began with a back.

Sienna Hart danced contemporary until a disc in her lower back said otherwise. A physical therapist sent her to a reformer; three months later she was walking without thinking about it, and a year later she was training to teach.

She opened Studio One in September 2020 with six reformers, north windows and one rule: every client is seen, every spring is set on purpose. Twelve people to a room is as big as it gets, so an instructor can always reach you in two steps.

Studio Two opened next door in 2023, with its wall of towers, and the first Teacher Training cohort started the same year. One of them now teaches the 6 am class.

Looking After the Room

Small things, done every class.

A reformer is a machine under load. We treat it like one.

  • Springs Swapped Yearly

    Every spring is checked each month and replaced every twelve, whether it looks tired or not.

  • Wiped After Every Class

    Carriages, blocks, straps and footbars, in the ten minutes between classes.

  • Grip Socks, Always

    For your footing on the bar and the upholstery's sake. A pair is $14 at the desk.
