North Fork Wind raises its first turbine.

Projects · September 24, 2026 · 4 min read — by Elise Kohler, Communications

Tower 1 of 145 now stands on the Medicine Bow ridge in Carbon County, Wyoming. It took the crew nine days from the first tower section to the last bolt on the third blade.

North Fork Wind is our largest project to date: 610 MW across 38,000 acres of leased ranch land, enough for about 200,000 homes once all 145 turbines turn. The county approved the permit in March 2025, and civil work — roads, foundations and the collector cable — began that summer.

Nine days, one crane

The main crane is a crawler that walks from pad to pad on its own tracks. It lifts four tower sections, the nacelle and then the hub with its three blades, each one 260 feet long. Wind is the limit: above about 20 miles an hour at the hook, the lift waits.

“We get maybe four calm mornings in a week up here. You plan for the wind, not against it.”Ryan Moe, North Fork construction manager

To make those mornings count, the blades travel at night from the rail yard in Rawlins, on county roads we rebuilt last year. That twelve-mile road project was paid by the North Fork community fund and is now open to everyone.

Local crews first

Of the 280 people on site this fall, 190 live in Carbon or a neighboring county. When the build ends, 22 technicians will stay on at the North Fork operations building outside Medicine Bow.

What comes next

Crews will raise two to three turbines a week through the fall, pause for the worst of the winter, and pick up again in March. The first 40 turbines should send power to the grid in spring 2027, the rest by year end.

Neighbors can follow progress at the monthly open evenings at the Medicine Bow community hall, or write to press@framorenewables.com.
