Case Study · Tendwell, 2027

A first week families actually finish.

Tendwell helps brothers and sisters share the care of an aging parent. Half of them gave up before the first shared task. Fourteen weeks later, most of them get there.

Client
Tendwell, Seattle
Role
Lead product designer
Timeline
14 weeks, Jan–Apr 2027
Platform
iOS and Android
Team
PM, 4 engineers, me

The Brief

Sign-up asked for everything before it gave anything.

Tendwell's first run asked a new caregiver for the parent's medications, doctors, insurance and the whole family's contacts before showing a single screen of value. 59% left on the way.

The team asked for a prettier form. We agreed on a different goal instead: get two siblings to share one real task in the first ten minutes.

Research

Twelve evenings on twelve sofas.

Caregivers open Tendwell after the kids are in bed, tired and on their phone. We watched twelve of them set it up at home, at the hour they really would.

“I downloaded it to split Mom's pharmacy runs with my brother. It asked me for her blood type.”

Interview 7, a caregiver in Tacoma

The New First Run

Eleven screens became five.

Name the parent, pick one task, invite one sibling. Medications and doctors wait until the family has a reason to add them.

Design Decisions

Three calls that moved the number.

  1. Value before data

    The first screen asks one question: who are you caring for? Everything else is optional until a task needs it. Required fields dropped from 23 to 2.

  2. The invite is the onboarding

    A shared task is only shared once a sibling joins. The invite became a step of its own, with a message the family can edit and send by text in one tap.

  3. A calm progress ring

    No streaks, no red badges. One ring shows how much of the week is covered, and who covered it. Caregivers told us it felt like help, not homework.

  4. Large type by default

    Many caregivers read on a phone held at arm's length after a long day. Body text went to 17 points and every tap target to 48 pixels.
