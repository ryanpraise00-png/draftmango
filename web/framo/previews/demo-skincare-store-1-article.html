How much sunscreen is enough? The two-finger rule, tested.

We measured what most of us put on against what the label was tested with. The gap is bigger than you think, and easy to close.

Theo BishopSkin Advice Lead · Mar 14, 2027

Share

Every sunscreen on our shelf was tested at the same amount: two milligrams for every square centimetre of skin. Almost nobody puts on that much. So we asked twelve people in the studio to apply their usual morning SPF, weighed what they used, and compared.

What the label was tested with

Two milligrams per square centimetre sounds abstract. For a face and neck it comes to about a quarter of a teaspoon, roughly 1.25 ml. Squeezed out of a tube, that is two lines the length of your index and middle fingers. That is the whole rule.

Our twelve volunteers used, on average, a little under half of it. The protection you get does not fall in a straight line with the amount, but it does fall: half the sunscreen gives you a good deal less than half the number on the front.

How to get there without feeling greasy

  1. Apply in two layers. One finger-length, spread it, wait a minute, then the second. Two thin layers sit better than one thick one.
  2. Make it your moisturiser. A fluid like Sheer Daylight SPF 50 hydrates enough for most skin; drop the cream underneath on oily days.
  3. Remember the edges. Hairline, ears, the sides of the neck: the places most often missed and most often burnt.
How much, where (at 2 mg per square centimetre)
Area Amount Looks like
Face and neck 1.25 ml Two finger-lengths
Each arm 2 ml A level teaspoon, a little under
Each leg 4 ml Two teaspoons
The whole body 30 ml A shot glass

“The best sunscreen is the one you like enough to use properly every morning. If it pills, shines or stings, you will quietly start using less.”

Theo Bishop, Skin Advice Lead

Reapplying, without starting over

Two hours outdoors, a swim or a sweaty walk home: that is when to reapply. Over makeup, a stick is the easiest way: three passes over each cheek, the nose and the forehead. Indoors, by a window, once in the morning is enough for most days.
