Do you need a battery in Phoenix?

Outages, time-of-use rates and what one 13.5 kWh unit actually keeps running on a July night.

Megan LoweHead of Design

Oct 8, 2026 · 6 min read

Most homes in the Valley don’t need a battery to save money. Plenty want one anyway. Here is how we help a family decide, with the numbers we use on the design visit.

A solar system without a battery does one job: it makes power while the sun is up and sends what you don’t use to the grid. When the grid goes down, it switches off too, so line workers can repair the street safely. That surprises people every summer.

Three reasons Valley homes add one

Outages. Monsoon storms take neighborhoods down for a few hours most Augusts. A battery keeps the fridge, lights, fans and Wi-Fi going without you touching anything; the switchover takes under a second.

Evening rates. Time-of-use plans charge the most from late afternoon into the evening, exactly when the panels fade. A battery stores the noon surplus and spends it after 4 pm.

Export rates. What utilities pay for the power you send them changes over time. Using more of your own power makes your savings less sensitive to that.

How big?

We size from a year of your usage, but these are the patterns we see most:

Units Stored Keeps running
One 13.5 kWh The essentials, through a night
Two 27 kWh The essentials plus one A/C zone
Three 40.5 kWh The whole house, A/C included

When we say skip it

If your roof is small, your evenings are quiet and outages don’t worry you, put the money into panels first. You can add a battery later in half a day, to our system or most others.

Incentives change. We check what applies to your home on the design visit and put it in writing; we never build a quote on a credit you might not get.

Want the numbers for your house? Send your last bill and we’ll price the system with and without a battery, side by side.
