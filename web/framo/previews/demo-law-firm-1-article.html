The new merger review rules: what changes for mid-market deals

The agencies have rewritten the premerger form and the review timetable. For deals between $120 million and $500 million, the filing now takes weeks, not days.

Catherine HaleChair, Corporate & M&A Daniel PricePartner, Corporate & M&A

6 min read

§Key Points

Filings now take 4 to 6 weeks to prepare, up from about one.

Buyers must describe overlaps and supply links, not only revenue codes.

Build the filing into the signing timetable, and say who pays for it.

For most mid-market deals, the premerger filing used to be an afterthought: a short form, a fee and a thirty-day wait. That changed on September 1. The new rules ask for more about the buyer, the target and the reasons for the deal, and they ask for it before the clock starts.

The waiting period itself is unchanged. What has changed is the work before it. In the first six weeks under the new form, the filings we prepared took between four and six weeks of drafting, against about one under the old one.

What the new form asks for

Three additions account for most of the extra time:

  • Overlap narratives. A written account of every product or service where the parties compete, with customers and rough shares.
  • Supply relationships. Where one party buys from or sells to the other, or to its competitors.
  • Deal documents. Board materials and drafts that discuss competition, prepared by officers and directors, not only final versions.

Who is affected

Every reportable deal files the new form. The burden falls hardest on buyers that have never filed before, and on sponsors whose portfolio companies overlap with the target.

Filing work, old form and new (our matters, Sep 1 to Oct 8, 2026)
Deal size Old form New form
$120M–$250M 4–6 days 3–4 weeks
$250M–$500M 1 week 4–5 weeks
Over $500M 1–2 weeks 5–6 weeks

The filing is now a workstream, with an owner and a date, not a form someone fills in after signing.

What to do now

  1. Start the overlap analysis when the letter of intent is signed, not when the purchase agreement is.
  2. Tell the deal team, and the board, that what they write about competitors may be filed.
  3. Set the filing deadline in the purchase agreement, and agree who bears the cost of the extra work.

If you are planning a deal for the first half of 2027, we are glad to walk your team through the form before the process starts.
