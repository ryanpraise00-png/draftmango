How to make your balayage last four months.

Color · By Ava Monroe, Creative Director · Sep 30, 2026 · 6 min read

A good balayage is designed to grow out. The lightness sits where the sun would put it, the roots stay soft, and there is no hard line at week six. But how long it stays bright depends on what happens at home.

Most of our balayage clients come back every three to four months; some stretch to five. Here is the routine we give them, in the order it matters.

1. Wait 72 hours before the first wash

Color keeps settling for a few days after you leave the chair. Skip the shampoo for three days, and when you do wash, use water that feels cool on your wrist. Hot water opens the hair and lets the tone slip.

2. Switch to a sulfate-free shampoo

Sulfates are what make a shampoo foam, and they are very good at lifting color. A sulfate-free formula cleans gently enough that the toner lasts weeks longer. Wash two or three times a week, not every day.

“The appointment people skip is the gloss at week eight. Thirty minutes, and it buys you another two months.”

3. Book a gloss halfway

Blonde lengths drift warm as the toner fades, especially in a Nashville summer. A gloss ($65 with a New Talent colorist, 30 minutes) resets the tone without lifting or touching the roots. It is the cheapest way to make the whole service last.

4. Protect it from heat and sun

Use a heat protectant every time you blow-dry or curl, and keep tools under 365°F. On sunny days and in the pool, a hat or a leave-in with UV filters does more than any purple shampoo.

The routine, in short

  • No shampoo for 72 hours, then cool water
  • Sulfate-free shampoo, two or three times a week
  • A gloss at week eight
  • Heat protectant, always; a hat in the sun
  • Purple shampoo once a week at most

If your color still turns brassy before eight weeks, tell us. Sometimes it is the water at home, sometimes the toner needs adjusting, and the fix is free within a month of your visit.
